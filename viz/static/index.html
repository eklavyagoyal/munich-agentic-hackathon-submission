CONTRACT EVIDENCE DICTIONARY

What every number means.

Definitions are deliberately conservative. A missing fact remains missing; a bound never becomes a point estimate; a transaction aggregate never becomes causal impact.

Decision geometry

a · issuer charge
Our submitted per-line-item charge in EUR. Charge sums describe submission shape, not tournament impact.
b · acceptance limit
Our reviewer threshold: accept an opponent charge when a ≤ b. A higher b can expose hidden rejected-fraud charges.
t_lo · proven floor
Sanctioned lower bound on secret fair value t, obtained only from tools/thresholds.py --jsonl.
t_hi · proven ceiling
Sanctioned upper evidence boundary. Missing means unknown—not zero, infinity, or “correct”.

Payoff and settlement

Issuer income
Sum of observed transaction payouts where Oasis is issuer, across all opponent reviewers. Denominator: one game or named item.
Reviewer cost
Observed Oasis settlement as reviewer. Rejected fair costs 1.5a; accepted fraud pays the observed capped amount.
Net score
issuer income − reviewer cost. Per-game calculations reconcile to official Oasis score within €0.01.
Gross observed flow
issuer income + reviewer cost per item. It excludes the unknowable attempted size of rejected fraud.

Proven valuation metrics

Proven income
16a only when a ≤ t_lo; zero only when a known t_hi proves a > t_hi. Everything between is excluded and counted.
Best possible
Σ 16t_lo over the same labelled denominator. Charging exactly the proven floor is certainly fair.
SCORE
proven income / best possible. This is a conservative valuation metric, not tournament score or realised euro impact.
Foregone lower bound
16 × (t_lo − a) only for proven undercharges. Unlabelled and open-side uncertainty receive no invented loss.

Observability states

Exact charge
A paid rejection proves fairness and exposes the submitted charge exactly.
Lower-bound charge
An accepted fraudulent settlement exposes payout, which may be capped below the original charge.
Hidden charge
Every reviewer rejected proven fraud, recording zero. The attempted amount cannot be recovered.
Unresolved
Available settlement evidence cannot prove fair or fraud. It stays outside the four proven cells.

Reviewer policy evidence

Reviewer cell
One recorded Oasis accept/reject decision for one opponent’s charge on one game/item. It is not an item, game, or euro denominator.
Limit audit
For an exact fair charge, the logged b is consistent only when charge ≤ b matches the recorded acceptance. Lower-bound and hidden fraud usually leave this relation indeterminate.
Priced policy Δ
Candidate reviewer-cost change only where visible evidence proves the candidate settlement. Both baseline and candidate use the same paired cells for the displayed scenario.
Potential hidden exposure
A rejected proven-fraud group with unknown charge where raised b exceeds t_lo. This proves only that exposure cannot be ruled out; it does not prove the charge crossed or assign euros.
Decision margin
(b − visible charge) / t_lo. It is exact for exact fair charges and only an upper bound for a fraudulent payout lower bound.
Isolated sensitivity
One sanctioned value bucket’s b shift varies while all other buckets stay at zero. It is retrospective partial-cost evidence, not a deployable policy search.

Operational forensics

Local-recorder status
Freshness of this repository’s append-only event file only. It never claims that the daemon or a separate submitting repository is healthy.
Played-game lag
Count of played games whose identifiers are later than the latest locally recorded pipeline game. It is not the same as all missing historical records.
Flight elapsed
Timestamp distance from the first logged round boundary. It is not CPU attribution or causal stage duration.
Reported ms
A generic event payload field retained under its neutral name because producer semantics are not proven.
Tier-one target
Whether the first observed tier-one send landed within 2 seconds of the first timestamped local boundary. This is an observed target, not a proven hard deadline.
Tier-two wall
Whether the first observed tier-two send landed within 52 seconds of the same local origin. Missing origin or send timing remains unobserved.
Stage envelope
P10, median, and P90 first-arrival times for each of ten core stages, each with its own timestamped-game denominator and a log-time display axis.
Recorder coverage
Observed core event types divided by ten expected types. Missing history is shown as absent, never reconstructed.
Missing boundary
No corresponding event exists in this local recorder. It does not prove a submission, case action, or tournament event failed to occur elsewhere.
Round replay boundary
One allow-listed milestone, belief, rule firing, or item decision at its logged sequence and timestamp. The replay state contains only events at or before the cursor.
Evidence-loss lens
An independently eligible game/item denominator for one missing or hidden artifact. Red means the selected evidence gap—not model error or causal loss.

Temporal monitoring contract

Prior-only baseline
For game k, only earlier observed games enter its centre and dispersion. The current game and all future games are excluded, including when values are missing.
Robust centre
The median of the selected earlier observations. A rolling window counts observed metric values, while source composition uses earlier played-game buckets.
Robust scale
The larger of 1.4826 × MAD and IQR / 1.349. Taking the larger dispersion estimate makes the signal band conservative when one estimator collapses.
Process signal
An observation whose absolute robust z strictly exceeds the selected 2, 3, or 4 sigma threshold. Direction becomes favourable or adverse from the metric contract; it is not a causal change point.
Zero-variance signal
The earlier window has no measurable dispersion but the current value differs. The UI reports that state explicitly instead of dividing by zero or inventing an infinite z-score.
Source-mix JSD
Jensen–Shannon divergence between current item-level belief-source shares and aggregated prior-window shares. It ranges from 0 for identical composition to 1 for disjoint composition; absent source labels stay visible as belief-missing.

Bounded belief calibration

Belief interval
The central lognormal interval median × exp(±zσ) in gross EUR, using the selected 50%, 80%, 90%, 95%, or 99% central mass.
Forced miss lower bound
The whole interval is below t_lo, or its lower endpoint is above a finite t_hi. Only those geometries prove the unknown fair value lies outside.
Compatible coverage upper bound
The interval is not forced to miss. This includes unresolved overlap and guaranteed capture, so it is an upper bound—not measured coverage.
Guaranteed capture lower bound
On the finite two-sided denominator only, the interval starts no higher than t_lo and ends no lower than t_hi, enclosing every value allowed by the bracket.
Nominal miss comparison
1 − central coverage. When the forced-miss lower bound already exceeds it, observed under-coverage is proven on this retrospective denominator; otherwise the bounds cannot decide.
Interval width
q_high / q_low = exp(2zσ). It measures multiplicative sharpness and can improve apparent compatibility simply by widening.

Two-role evidence cartography

Issuer shortfall component
The item’s foregone-income lower bound: 16 × (t_lo − a) only when the Oasis charge is provably under the sanctioned floor.
Wrong-review component
Observed Oasis settlement EUR from proven reject-fair and accept-fraud reviewer groups joined to that same game/item identifier.
Additive evidence load
Issuer shortfall lower bound plus observed wrong-review cost. This transparent index supports triage; it is not avoidable tournament loss or proof both components could be recovered together.
Same-item collision
An item with a positive issuer shortfall lower bound and positive observed wrong-review cost. It is identifier overlap, not causal coupling.
Hidden fraud group
A proven fraudulent opponent charge Oasis rejected whose attempted size remains invisible. Its count is retained; no euro value is reconstructed.
Taxonomy cell
Exactly one issuer evidence state crossed with exactly one reviewer error direction. Cells partition the selected item universe and reconcile to game and item ledgers.
Routing-cell intensity
The selected focus value divided by that source × floor-regime cell’s item count. Total focused value, collision rate, and hidden-group count remain alongside it so per-item burden cannot be mistaken for total scale.
Routing recurrence warning
“Sparse” means fewer than five items or three represented games; “game-concentrated” means one game carries over 65% of focused value; “limited recurrence” means fewer than three games have positive focused value. “Repeated” clears only those descriptive warnings—it is not validation.

Item peer forensics

Peer contract
An exact retrospective filter: same logged source plus sanctioned-floor regime, same source only, or same floor regime only. It never uses claim-text similarity.
Strictly prior peer
A matching item with a lower game identifier than the selected item. The selected game and every later game are excluded from prior summaries.
All peer
Every matching item except the selected item. Same-game and future rows are hindsight context and are rendered separately.
Empirical midrank
(values below + 0.5 × ties) / observed peers. Every metric carries its own non-missing denominator.
Peer envelope
P10, median, and P90 of observed peer values. It is descriptive and uses a row-local raw-unit scale—not a prediction or confidence interval.
Numeric analogue
Robust-scaled distance over charge/floor log, belief/floor log, normalised b−floor, and sigma, requiring at least two common dimensions. The nearest-prior calculation derives scales from prior peers only; the full ledger is explicitly hindsight. Proximity is not semantic substitutability.

Comparative analytics

Pareto frontier
A game is non-dominated when no observed game has at least as much income and no more reviewer cost, with one strict improvement.
Shock-adjusted standings
Descriptive lenses applied independently to each team’s rounds. They are neither alternate tournament scores nor forecasts.
Joint-round bootstrap
One complete historical game vector containing all seventeen teams is sampled per future game. Teams are never sampled independently, so observed same-round shocks remain coupled.
Finish cone
Pointwise percentiles across seeded bootstrap paths from the latest materialised score through game 100. It is conditional on the selected evidence window, not a confidence interval for an unknown true forecast.
Monte Carlo margin
The worst-case 95% binomial sampling margin, 1.96 × 0.5 / √paths. It measures simulation noise only—not model error or future uncertainty.
Temporal reality check
At each historical cutoff, only earlier complete round vectors predict the next round. P10–P90 coverage evaluates score intervals; Brier score evaluates the event “Oasis improves rank”, with a constant empirical event-rate baseline.
Opponent aggression
Median observable charge divided by proven floor. Hidden charges are excluded and coverage is always reported.
SHADOW candidate
A logged decision not submitted. Only exact logged from → to pairs enter counterfactual charts.
Firing denominator
One logged rule event. The same rule may appear more than once for the same game/item, so this denominator cannot be treated as an item portfolio.
Portfolio denominator
One latest-sequence candidate per rule × game × item. A routed fallback keeps the logged before decision; all SCORE bars retain the full labelled-primary denominator.

Cohort research contract

Cohort denominator
One filtered set of game × item identifiers. Its fixed valuation denominator is Σ16t_lo over decided items with a sanctioned floor; excluded charges remain in that denominator.
Cohort SCORE difference
Cohort B SCORE minus Cohort A SCORE. Each cohort has its own fixed denominator, so the difference can reflect item mix and is not a treatment effect.
Game-cluster bootstrap
Whole games—not individual items or review cells—are resampled with replacement. Identical game sets reuse the same sampled indexes (paired); different sets are sampled independently.
Item-universe overlap
Jaccard overlap of selected game × item identifiers. It describes membership only and does not turn repeated or different-game items into matched observations.

Market microstructure

Directed edge
One ordered issuer → reviewer relationship. Self-review cells are structurally ineligible; the matrix has 272 eligible edges for 17 teams.
Temporal edge frame
One directed edge in one played game. Cumulative replay sums frames only through its cursor; single-round replay uses exactly the selected game.
Penalty wedge
reviewer settlement − issuer payout on the same edge. A fair rejection creates a positive wedge because the reviewer pays 1.5a while issuer accounting records its own payout.
Bilateral net
Selected-team issuer income from a counterparty minus selected-team reviewer cost to that counterparty. It is observed score accounting, not proof of strategic exploitation.
Market reconciliation
Every edge frame rolls up to the all-history edge and every frame rolls up to its team/game timeline exactly. The residual against displayed official scores is reported separately because per-round display rounding can accumulate.

Paired rival accounting

Matched game
One played game with an observed market timeline row for both Oasis and the selected rival. No unmatched round enters the comparison.
Income edge
Oasis issuer income − rival issuer income on the selected matched-game denominator. Positive helps Oasis.
Reviewer-cost edge
rival reviewer cost − Oasis reviewer cost on the same games. Positive means Oasis paid less.
Paired score gap
The exact market-accounting identity income edge + reviewer-cost edge = Oasis score − rival score. A separate total and maximum-per-game residual audit the rounded official display.
Absolute gap flow
Σ |same-game score gap|. Concentration shares describe how much of that absolute movement sits in the largest one or five games; they are not euro impact beyond score.
Paired bootstrap
A seeded resample of complete matched games. The interval describes historical mean-gap sampling instability under that empirical game set—not future performance or causal advantage.
All-history rank
Rank from the complete materialized market summary. It stays all-history even when the studio’s paired accounting window is narrowed.
